Australia Post: From Letters to Deliveries

Digital communication reduced the need for physical letters, while digital commerce increased the importance of physical delivery.

01 LETTERS

Fewer Letters, but More Addresses to Serve

Australians are sending fewer letters, but Australia Post is serving more delivery points. This creates a growing mismatch between its traditional letter business and the size of the network it must maintain.

The Postal Paradox: Fewer Letters, More Delivery Points

Addressed letters (billion) Delivery points (million)

Declining letter demand does not necessarily reduce delivery obligations.

As digital communication replaces some physical correspondence, Australia Post still needs to maintain delivery coverage across a growing number of addresses. With fewer letters distributed across more delivery points, the network may face higher delivery costs per letter and greater pressure to operate efficiently.

The Changing Mix of Australian Letters

The composition of addressed letters has shifted over time, with some letter categories becoming more prominent than others.

2006/07 2023/24

PreSort Small letters became more dominant in the addressed-letter mix.

PreSort Small letters increased from 45% to 65% of the addressed-letter mix between 2006/07 and 2023/24, while ordinary small letters declined from 40% to 24%.

Australia Post's Two-Speed Business

Letters consistently recorded expenditure above revenue, while Non-Letter recorded revenue above expenditure.

Revenue Expenditure

Australia Post's traditional letters business faces financial pressure, while non-letter services show a different picture.

From FY2021 to FY2025, letter expenditure consistently exceeded revenue, indicating ongoing financial pressure. In contrast, non-letter services generated revenue above expenditure throughout the period. This highlights the growing importance of non-letter operations to Australia Post's overall business.

02 COMMERCE

Meanwhile, Online Shopping Is Expanding

At the same time, Australians are increasingly shopping online. This does not explain the decline in letters, but it creates a different type of demand for Australia Post's delivery network. The next charts show how online shopping has grown and which categories are driving that demand.

Australia's Shift to Online Shopping

Online retail has become a larger part of Australian retail spending, although its growth has not been uniform over time.

Online shopping has become a lasting part of retail spending.

Online retail's share increased from 2.1% in July 2013 to 12.7% in June 2025. The sharp rise around 2020–2021, followed by continued relatively high levels, shows that online shopping remained more prominent even after its earlier surge.

Food and non-food retailing experienced their largest monthly surges at different times.

Online non-food retailing rose by 38.2% in April 2020, while online food retailing increased by 27.1% in May 2020. These peaks show that different retail categories experienced unusually large monthly changes at different points during the early pandemic period.

03 GEOGRAPHY

But Delivery Demand Is Not Growing Evenly

National growth tells only part of the story. Moving from state-level spending to local shopping hotspots and parcel growth reveals how delivery demand varies across Australia.

Where Is Online Spending Concentrated?

Compare total online spending across Australian states and territories.

How to read this map: Colours represent total spending, not spending per person.

The largest online shopping markets are not necessarily the fastest-growing.

New South Wales ($26.4 billion) and Victoria ($20.0 billion) lead in total online spending. However, Western Australia recorded the highest year-on-year spending growth at 18% in this dataset. This shows that market size and growth rate tell different stories about online shopping demand.

Parcel Growth Rates Vary Across Australia

Compare percentage growth in parcel volumes across Australian states and territories. Larger circles indicate higher growth rates, not greater total parcel volumes.

Parcel growth is uneven across Australian states and territories

The Northern Territory (12.4%) and Tasmania (11.4%) recorded the highest parcel-volume growth rates in this dataset, while New South Wales (5.2%) and Victoria (5.8%) recorded more moderate increases. This highlights regional differences in the pace of parcel growth, rather than the total number of parcels handled.

04 NETWORK

The Postal Network Is Changing With Delivery Demand

These changes in delivery demand also affect the network needed to serve Australians. Australia Post is expanding parcel-oriented infrastructure while continuing to maintain an extensive network of delivery points.

Where are Australia's Postal Facilities Located?

Explore the geagraphic distribution of over 8600 OpenStreetMap-recorded post offices and post boxes across Australia.

Post office Post box

Postal facilities are concentrated in Australia's more populated regions.

Post offices and post boxes are more densely distributed along Australia's eastern and southeastern coasts, particularly around major urban areas. In contrast, inland regions have fewer recorded facilities. This highlights the uneven geographic distribution of postal infrastructure and the challenge of maintaining access across Australia's vast territory.

Parcel Lockers Expand as Traditional Postal Infrastructure Declines

Australia Post's infrastructure mix is changing.

Parcel-locker expansion alongside fewer street posting boxes suggests a shift in the types of postal services being provided. However, the continued growth in delivery points shows the Australia Post must still maintain broad delivery coverage.

05 FUTURE

The Transformation Is Not Over

Australia Post's shift towards delivery is still unfolding. Even as parcels become more important to the network, letter volumes are projected to continue falling, extending the challenge that began this story.

The Decline of Letters Is Not Over

Projected letter volumes continue to decline across all forecast scenarios

Letter volumes are expected to keep falling, increasing pressure on Australia Post's delivery network.

The mean forecast shows letter volumes declining by approximately 27%, from 1,048 million in FY2025–26 to 770 million in FY2028–29. Even under the optimistic scenario, letter volumes are projected to decrease. This suggests that Australia Post will need to continue adapting its network to handle fewer letters while meeting growing delivery needs.
