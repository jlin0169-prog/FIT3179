From Letters to Deliveries

Fewer letters. More parcels. A postal network being reshaped.

Australia Post has connected Australians for generations. But the way we communicate and shop is changing.

As traditional letters decline and online shopping expands, Australia Post faces a growing challenge: serving more addresses, responding to changing parcel demand and maintaining access to essential services across the country.

How is Australia's postal network adapting to this transformation?

Explore the trends behind the shift from traditional mail to modern delivery services.

01 THE PARADOX

Why Is Australia Post Delivering Fewer Letters but Serving More Addresses?

The Postal Paradox: Fewer Letters, More Delivery Points

Addressed letters (billion) Delivery points (million)

As electronic communication replaces traditional mail, Australia's addressed letter volumes fell by 38% between 2019 and 2024, while delivery points increased to 12.7 million. This widening gap puts pressure on delivery costs, as fewer letters must be delivered across a growing network.

To adapt, Australia Post introduced every-second-business-day delivery for regular letters in 2024, reflecting its response to declining demand.

From Physical Letters to Digital Communication

By 2023, 78% of Australian adults used messaging apps, while physical letter volumes continued to decline, falling by approximately 6% that year.

This contrast reflects a broader shift towards digital communication, as everyday messages, bills and notices increasingly move online, reducing reliance on traditional mail.

However, fewer letters do not necessarily mean less demand for Australia Post's services.

Australia Post's Two-Speed Business

Revenue Expenditure

Australia Post's Letters business remained loss-making throughout FY2021-FY2025. In FY2025, its expenditure exceeded revenue by approximately $230.4 million.

Meanwhile, non-letter operations recorded a surplus of approximately $313.6 million.

The contrast reveals why Australia Post cannot rely on its traditional letters business alone. As letter demand weakens, parcels and other services play an increasingly important role in supporting the organisation.

So, what is creating this new demand?

02 THE SHIFT

If Letters Are Declining, What's Driving Delivery Demand?

Online Retail Becomes a Larger Share of Australian Spending

First COVID-19 impact (Apr 2020) Delta lockdowns (Aug-Sep 2021)

Following the first COVID-19 lockdowns in March 2020, online retail's share jumped from 7.1% to 11.1% in April, as restrictions encouraged consumers to shop online.

During the 2021 Delta outbreaks, extended lockdowns in major cities further disrupted in-store shopping, coinciding with online retail's highest share of 15.3% in September 2021.

Even after restrictions eased, online retail remained above pre-pandemic levels, reaching 12.7% in June 2025, reflecting a lasting shift towards online shopping.

The first COVID-19 lockdowns triggered sharp increases in online shopping, with non-food retail surging 38.2% in April 2020, followed by 27.1% growth in online food retail in May.

These uneven spikes suggest that shopping habits shifted at different speeds across categories. Although monthly growth remained volatile, online shopping became a more established part of Australian retail.

But did this growing delivery demand spread evenly across Australia?

03 THE GEOGRAPHY

Is Australia's Parcel Boom Happening Everywhere?

Where Is Online Spending Concentrated?

New South Wales leads Australia's online shopping market with $26.4 billion in spending, followed by Victoria at $20.0 billion. However, the Australian Capital Territory records the highest estimated spending per resident at $3,529, compared with $3,089 in NSW.

This contrast shows that larger populations contribute to higher total spending, while spending per resident reveals differences in shopping activity.

But do the largest online shopping markets also experience the fastest growth?

Parcel Volume Growth Varies Across Australia

While NSW and Victoria dominate total online spending, the Northern Territory (12.4%) and Tasmania (11.4%) recorded faster online purchase growth than NSW (5.2%) and Victoria (5.8%) during the 2024 peak shopping period

Major sales events such as Black Friday and Cyber Monday helped drive nationwide shopping activity. Australia Post delivered a record 102.8 million parcels during November-December 2024, up 3.1% year-on-year, highlighting the growing importance of parcel delivery across Australia.

04 THE RESPONSE

How Is Australia Post Reshaping Its Network?

Where Is Australia's Postal Network Concentrated?

Source: OpenStreetMap contributors, accessed via Overpass API (October 2026). Data processed using Python.

Australia's post offices and post boxes are heavily concentrated along the eastern and southeastern coasts, particularly around major cities, while inland regions have far fewer mapped facilities.

This uneven distribution reflects Australia's population geography but also highlights the challenge of maintaining nationwide postal accessibility. As parcel demand grows and traditional letter volumes decline, Australia Post must balance network efficiency with access to essential services across regional and remote communities.

The 18.9% increase in parcel locker banks reflects Australia Post's growing focus on convenient parcel collection. With 24/7 access, parcel lockers allow customers to collect online purchases at their preferred time, reducing the inconvenience of missed home deliveries.

Meanwhile, the 15.6% decline in street posting boxes is consistent with falling demand for traditional letters. This shift highlights how Australia Post is adapting its infrastructure to changing customer habits, while still needing to maintain essential postal services across Australia.

Post Offices Play Different Roles Across Australia

Postage Financial services Identity services Other services Consumer products

In major cities, postage accounts for 38% of post-office transactions, but only 22% in very remote areas. In contrast, financial services make up 45% of transactions in very remote communities, compared with 32% in major cities.

This difference shows that post offices are more than places to send letters and parcels. In remote communities, they also provide access to essential financial services where banking options may be limited. As Australia Post expands its parcel infrastructure, maintaining these community services remains just as important as modernising deliveries.

05 THE FUTURE

Can Australia Post Keep Up With a Changing Australia?

The Decline of Letters Is Not Over

Letter volumes are projected to fall by another 27% by FY2028-29, from 1,048 million to just 770 million under the mean forecast. Even the optimistic scenario predicts continued decline, suggesting that traditional mail is unlikely to recover.

With fewer letters being delivered, the cost of serving each address may rise, particularly as the number of delivery points continues to grow. This could place further pressure on Australia Post's already loss-making Letters business, making improvements in delivery efficiency increasingly important.

The Mailbox Is Changing - Not Disappearing.

Australia Post's transformation is not simply a story of letters being replaced by parcels. Letter volumes are declining even as delivery points grow. Meanwhile, online shopping is expanding, regional demand is changing, and postal infrastructure is adapting to new delivery and collection needs.

The challenge ahead is to adapt to changing demands while continuing to serve communities across Australia.
