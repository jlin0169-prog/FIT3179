From Letters to Deliveries

Fewer letters. More addresses. A postal network in transition.

As Australians send fewer letters and shop more online, Australia Post faces changing delivery demands. Explore how these shifts are reshaping postal services, regional demand and infrastructure.

01 LETTERS

Fewer Letters, but More Addresses to Serve

The Postal Paradox: Fewer Letters, More Delivery Points

Addressed letters (billion) Delivery points (million)

Due to electronic substitution, Australia Post is handling fewer letters each year. Yet the number of delivery points continues to grow, meaning its postal network must serve more addresses despite declining letter demand.

This widening gap puts pressure on delivery costs and efficiency. To adapt, Australia Post introduced every-second-business-day delivery for regular letters in 2024, reflecting how the postal service is responding to changing communication habits.

From Physical Letters to Digital Communication

By 2023, messaging-app usage had reached approximately 78% of Australian adults, reflecting how widely digital communication had become part of everyday life. Meanwhile, physical letter volumes continued to decline, highlighting the growing contrast between digital communication and traditional mail.

Interestingly, messaging-app usage remained relatively stable after 2023, but letter volumes continued falling. This suggests that the decline in physical correspondence is part of a broader digital transition, involving not only messaging apps but also online billing, banking and electronic services.

Australia Post's Two-Speed Business

Revenue Expenditure

Australia Post's financial performance reveals a growing divide between its services. Despite changes in postal demand, the Letters business remained loss-making throughout FY2021-FY2025, reflecting the financial pressure of maintaining traditional mail services.

In contrast, non-letter operations consistently generated positive results. By FY2025, Letters recorded a $230.4 million loss, while Non-Letters delivered a $313.6 million surplus. This contrast highlights the importance of Australia Post's broader parcel and services operations as traditional letter demand declines.

02 COMMERCE

Meanwhile, Online Shopping Is Expanding

Online Retail Becomes a Larger Share of Australian Spending

Online shopping has become an increasingly important part of Australian retail. The share of retail spending online rose from 2.1% in July 2013 to 12.7% in June 2025, with a particularly sharp increase during 2020-2021, when pandemic restrictions accelerated the shift towards online purchasing.

Although online retail's share eased from its earlier peaks, it remained well above pre-pandemic levels. For Australia Post, this shift provides an important context for the growing role of parcel delivery and e-commerce logistics, even as traditional letter volumes continue to decline.

The early pandemic brought sharp but uneven changes in online shopping. Online non-food retailing surged by 38.2% in April 2020, followed by a 27.1% increase in online food retailing in May 2020.

These peaks highlight how different retail categories responded at different times. Yet the frequent increases and decreases across later years also reveal that online retail growth was far from steady. While online shopping became a larger part of Australian retail, its month-to-month performance remained volatile.

03 GEOGRAPHY

But Delivery Demand Is Not Growing Evenly

Where Is Online Spending Concentrated?

The largest online shopping market is not necessarily the highest-spending per resident. New South Wales leads in total online spending at $26.4 billion, followed by Victoria at $20.0 billion. However, the Australian Capital Territory records the highest spending per resident at $3,529, compared with $3,089 in New South Wales.

This contrast reveals how population size influences total market spending, while per-resident figures offer a different perspective on regional online shopping activity.

Parcel Growth Rates Vary Across Australia

Parcel growth is not evenly distributed across Australia. The Northern Territory (12.4%) and Tasmania (11.4%) recorded the highest parcel-volume growth rates, while New South Wales (5.2%) and Victoria (5.8%) experienced more moderate increases.

This contrast shows that regions with smaller online shopping markets can experience faster parcel growth, highlighting differences in the pace of change across Australia. However, higher percentage growth does not necessarily mean a greater number of parcels delivered.

04 NETWORK

The Postal Network Is Changing With Delivery Demand

Where Is Australia's Postal Network Concentrated?

Mapped post offices and post boxes cluster along the eastern and southeastern coasts, particularly around major cities, while inland Australia has a much sparser distribution.

This pattern illustrates the challenge of maintaining postal access across Australia's vast geography. As traditional mail volumes decline and parcel demand changes, Australia Post faces the task of balancing nationwide accessibility with an evolving delivery network.

From Street Posting Boxes to Parcel Lockers

From FY2024 to FY2025, parcel locker banks increased by 18.9%, while street posting boxes declined by 15.6%. Post offices also decreased by 1.9%, whereas delivery points continued to grow slightly by 0.8%.

These contrasting trends suggest that Australia Post is adapting its infrastructure to changing delivery needs, while maintaining an extensive delivery network.

Post Offices Play Different Roles Across Australia

In major cities, accounted for 38% of post-office transactions, compared with 22% in very remote areas. By contrast, financial services represented 45% of transactions in very remote Australia, compared with 32% in major cities.

This contrast highlights how post offices provide more than mail services, particularly in remote communities where financial services account for a larger share of transactions.

05 FUTURE

The Transformation Is Not Over

The Decline of Letters Is Not Over

The mean forecast projects a 27% decline in letter volumes, from 1,048 million in FY2025-26 to 770 million in FY2028-29. Even under the optimistic scenario, letter volumes are expected to continue falling.

This reinforces the challenge facing Australia Post: maintaining nationwide postal access while adapting its network to changing demand. As traditional mail declines, the expansion of parcel lockers and other delivery infrastructure illustrates how the postal network is evolving.

THE BIG PICTURE

The Mailbox Is Changing - Not Disappearing.

Australia Post's transformation is not simply a story of letters being replaced by parcels. Letter volumes are declining even as delivery points grow. Meanwhile, online shopping is expanding, regional demand is changing, and postal infrastructure is adapting to new delivery and collection needs.

The challenge ahead is to adapt to changing demands while continuing to serve communities across Australia.
